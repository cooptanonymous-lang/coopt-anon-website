CO-OPT: Lifelong Learning of Object-Oriented Preferences for Personalized Robotic Assistance

Anonymous Authors

Abstract

For safe and fluent human-robot interaction (HRI), robotic assistants must not only complete tasks but do so while respecting their user's preferences. In lifelong deployment scenarios, these preferences are often not all readily apparent and may surface in different situations. We study how a robot can continually learn these preferences while minimizing the total effort spent by the user in communicating them.

First, we formalize the problem of lifelong learning of object-oriented preferences (LOOP), where a robot assists the user over a sequence of tasks and learns how objects should be handled from corrections. Next, we present CO-OPT, a continual learning approach that estimates user preferences about objects from language corrections to generate user-aligned plans, and apply it to household cleanup tasks with a mobile manipulator.

In both simulation experiments and a real-world user study (N=16), CO-OPT reduces the corrections needed to align robot behavior. Participant responses further show that CO-OPT significantly (p < 0.05) improves their acceptance of the robot and the fluency of the resulting interaction. These results indicate that transferring learned preferences across tasks and objects lowers user burden while improving interaction, a prerequisite for robots to remain useful in long-term HRI.
