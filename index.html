CO-OPT: Lifelong Learning of Object-Oriented Preferences for Personalized Robotic Assistance

Under Review

Anonymous Authors

Abstract

For safe and fluent human-robot interaction (HRI), robotic assistants must not only complete tasks but do so while respecting their user's preferences. In lifelong deployment scenarios, these preferences are often not all readily apparent and may surface in different situations. We study how a robot can continually learn these preferences while minimizing the total effort spent by the user in communicating them.

First, we formalize the problem of lifelong learning of object-oriented preferences (LOOP), where a robot assists the user over a sequence of tasks and learns how objects should be handled from corrections. Next, we present CO-OPT, a continual learning approach that estimates user preferences about objects from language corrections to generate user-aligned plans, and apply it to household cleanup tasks with a mobile manipulator.

In both simulation experiments and a real-world user study (N=16), CO-OPT reduces the corrections needed to align robot behavior. Participant responses further show that CO-OPT significantly (p < 0.05) improves their acceptance of the robot and the fluency of the resulting interaction. These results indicate that transferring learned preferences across tasks and objects lowers user burden while improving interaction, a prerequisite for robots to remain useful in long-term HRI.

Post-Experiment Survey Questions

Below were the questions used in the post-experiment survey taken by the participants at the end of the study. For pages 1 to 3, participants scored each item on a scale from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 7 (Strongly Agree).


Page 1 of 4

    Carefully read each statement and indicate how strongly you agree or disagree with the statement.

    1. I trusted the robot to do the right thing at the right time.
    2. The robot was trustworthy.
    3. The human-robot team improved over time.
    4. The human-robot team’s fluency improved over time.
    5. The robot’s performance improved over time.
    6. The robot perceives accurately what my goals are.
    7. The robot does not understand what I am trying to accomplish.

    Imagine you are working from home, doing computer-based tasks in a household environment. Based on this scenario, carefully read each statement and indicate how strongly you agree or disagree with the statement.

    1. Using this robot would enable me to accomplish tasks more quickly.
    2. Using this robot would increase my productivity.
    3. I would find it easy to get the robot to do what I want it to do.
    4. I would find the robot easy to use.

    In this section of the survey, you will be shown user preferences about robot behavior that appeared in the study. For each preference, indicate how strongly you agree or disagree with the following statement:

    “If I asked the robot to perform the tasks again, it would respect this preference.”

    1. Produce items should be put in the trash bin.
    2. Produce items should be picked up first.
    3. Cups, cans, and bottles should be put in the recycling bin.
    4. Cups, cans, and bottles should be picked up last.
    5. The robot should not pick up electronic devices.
    6. The robot should pick up produce items more quickly.
    7. The robot should pick up cups, cans, and bottles more slowly.
    8. The robot should not go in front of the TV unless it absolutely has to.

    Answer these short response questions to provide feedback about your experience today. Although you are free to answer however you want, do your best to elaborate upon your answers.

    1. In this experiment, you communicated your preferences to the robot using the provided user interface. What additional features would you like this interface to have?
    2. In this experiment, you taught the robot your preferences by describing them in plain language through chat. If the system could be extended with entirely new ways of teaching the robot, what would you want them to be?
    3. (optional) Do you have any other comments about your experience: anything you liked, found frustrating, or would change about the robot, the tasks, or the study itself?
